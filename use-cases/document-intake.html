USE CASE 01 · SURVEILLANCE & INFORMATICS

Case and laboratory document intake

Convert incoming reports into review-ready surveillance records while preserving the original source and human validation.

Related Azure AI App TemplateMulti-modal Content Processing

Extracts information from unstructured documents and maps it to a structured format.

Open GitHub repository View catalog entry

Imagine when the statewide morning intake queue already has 400 reports.

Structured ELR messages are mixed with faxed case-report forms and PDF laboratory attachments from dozens of providers and jurisdictions. Investigations cannot start until the unstructured records are classified, abstracted, and matched to the right person or case.

Business need and data challenge

The bottleneck is not a lack of data; it is variation in format and quality. ELR may arrive as HL7 v2 or FHIR, eCR as CDA or FHIR, while provider forms arrive as low-resolution images with missing fields, handwriting, duplicate patients, and inconsistent dates.

The business need is to shorten receipt-to-assignment time without allowing uncertain extraction to become accepted surveillance data.

How this applies across the broader workflow

The interface first routes valid HL7 or FHIR messages through existing interoperability rules. Only fax and PDF content is sent to document classification and extraction. The model proposes fields such as patient name, date of birth, reportable condition, onset date, ordering provider, specimen date, LOINC-coded test, and result.

The original image and proposed values appear together in an intake work queue. Staff resolve duplicate-person matches, correct low-confidence fields, and approve the record before the surveillance platform creates or updates the case. Corrections become quality metrics for each sender, document type, and field.

Data challenges to resolve

  • Sender-specific layouts, low-resolution scans, handwriting, and weak OCR quality.
  • Missing or conflicting identifiers, dates, condition names, and specimen details.
  • Duplicate-person and duplicate-case matching across structured and unstructured reports.

Required guardrails

  • Process protected health information only in approved environments.
  • Require authorized staff acceptance before finalizing a record.
  • Keep every proposed value traceable to the original source.
  • Monitor correction rates by sender, document type, and field.

Reflection questions

  • Which fields delay assignment most often?
  • What confidence threshold should require manual review?
  • How will duplicate-person and duplicate-case matching be handled?
