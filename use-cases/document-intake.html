DISEASE SURVEILLANCE

eCR, ELR, and surveillance intake

Route structured eCR and ELR, then extract fields from fax, PDF, and other surveillance intake formats.

Imagine when the statewide morning intake queue already has 400 reports.

Structured eCR documents containing clinical narrative and ELR messages containing laboratory results arrive alongside faxed case-report forms, PDF laboratory attachments, and secure-email referrals from dozens of providers and jurisdictions. Some eCR and ELR submissions fail validation or include attachments that still require review before investigations can begin.

Business need and data challenge

The bottleneck is not a lack of data; it is variation in format and quality. eCR commonly sends an electronic initial case report (eICR) as HL7 CDA R2 XML and receives a Reportability Response, with FHIR eCR emerging where supported. ELR commonly arrives as HL7 v2 ORU messages, with FHIR DiagnosticReport and Observation used in some implementations. Provider forms and attachments may still arrive as low-resolution images with missing fields, handwriting, duplicate patients, and inconsistent dates.

The business need is to shorten receipt-to-assignment time without allowing failed structured messages or uncertain extraction to become accepted surveillance data.

Where the accelerator fits

Use it for unstructured supplements and exception documents.

Multi-modal Content Processing does not parse or validate eICR CDA XML, HL7 v2 ELR, FHIR resources, or Reportability Responses. It fits after the interface and rules layer has separated valid structured messages from fax and PDF reports, eCR attachments, scanned clinical documents, and exception artifacts that require extraction. It classifies those unstructured items, proposes surveillance fields, and sends values with confidence and source location into the staff validation queue.

Reuse: the upload experience, document classification, multimodal extraction, structured output, and review pattern.

Adapt: the document schemas, surveillance field definitions, confidence thresholds, protected-data boundary, matching service, and API used to create a draft record.

Keep outside: eICR CDA/RR, ELR HL7/FHIR transport and conformance validation, reportability rules, identity matching, final case creation, and acceptance by authorized staff.

Potential outcomes

  • Faster, more complete intake across eCR, ELR, fax, PDF, and referral channels.
  • Less repetitive abstraction while preserving authorized review.
  • Measurable sender-, format-, and field-level data-quality trends.

How this addresses the challenge

Valid eCR and ELR stay in established interoperability workflows, failed structured messages move to an exception queue, and only unstructured records and attachments use AI extraction. Confidence thresholds, required-field rules, duplicate checks, and side-by-side source review prevent uncertain values from silently becoming accepted case data.

How this applies across the broader workflow

  • Validate eICR CDA/RR, FHIR eCR where supported, and ELR HL7 v2 or FHIR through the interface, conformance, terminology, and reportability-rules layer.
  • Route failed structured messages to technical exception handling and send fax, PDF, scanned forms, secure-email documents, and eCR attachments to the document accelerator with source, error, and confidence details.
  • Have staff resolve person and case matches, approve proposed fields, create or update the surveillance record, and use corrections as sender-, format-, and field-level quality measures.
