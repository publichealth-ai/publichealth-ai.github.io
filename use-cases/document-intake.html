DISEASE SURVEILLANCE

eCR, ELR, and surveillance intake

Route structured eCR and ELR, then extract fields from fax, PDF, and other surveillance intake formats.

Imagine when the statewide morning intake queue already has 400 reports.

Structured eCR documents containing clinical narrative and ELR messages containing laboratory results arrive alongside faxed case-report forms, PDF laboratory attachments, and secure-email referrals from dozens of providers and jurisdictions. Some eCR and ELR submissions fail validation or include attachments that still require review before investigations can begin.

Business need and data challenge

The bottleneck is not a lack of data; it is variation in format and quality. ELR may arrive as HL7 v2 or FHIR, eCR as CDA or FHIR, while provider forms arrive as low-resolution images with missing fields, handwriting, duplicate patients, and inconsistent dates.

The business need is to shorten receipt-to-assignment time without allowing uncertain extraction to become accepted surveillance data.

Where the accelerator fits

Use it for the unstructured and exception-handling step, not the entire intake pipeline.

Multi-modal Content Processing fits after routing and validation distinguish processable eCR and ELR from failed messages, fax and PDF reports, and document attachments. It classifies the unstructured item, extracts defined surveillance fields, and sends proposed values with confidence and source location into the staff validation queue.

Reuse: the upload experience, document classification, multimodal extraction, structured output, and review pattern.

Adapt: the eCR and ELR exception rules, document schemas, surveillance field definitions, confidence thresholds, protected-data boundary, matching service, and API used to create a draft record.

Keep outside: eCR and ELR transport and conformance validation, enterprise identity matching, final case creation, and acceptance by authorized staff.

Potential outcomes

  • Faster, more complete intake across eCR, ELR, fax, PDF, and referral channels.
  • Less repetitive abstraction while preserving authorized review.
  • Measurable sender-, format-, and field-level data-quality trends.

How this addresses the challenge

Valid eCR and ELR stay in established interoperability workflows, failed structured messages move to an exception queue, and only unstructured records and attachments use AI extraction. Confidence thresholds, required-field rules, duplicate checks, and side-by-side source review prevent uncertain values from silently becoming accepted case data.

How this applies across the broader workflow

  • Validate ELR delivered through HL7 v2 or FHIR and eCR delivered through CDA or FHIR; route valid content through established interoperability and business rules.
  • Move failed eCR and ELR, fax, PDF, secure-email documents, and eCR attachments into one exception and extraction queue with source, error, and confidence details.
  • Have staff resolve person and case matches, approve proposed fields, create or update the surveillance record, and use corrections as sender-, format-, and field-level quality measures.

Reflection questions

  • Which report types and surveillance fields create the longest delay today, and what baseline measures—receipt-to-assignment time, backlog size, correction rate—will show whether the workflow improves?
  • What confidence thresholds, required-field rules, and sender-specific exceptions should route a document to manual abstraction rather than allowing a proposed value into the approval queue?
  • How will identity matching and case deduplication work across ELR, eCR, fax, and PDF sources, and who resolves a match when the available identifiers conflict?
