Emergency knowledge and policy assistant

Find the plans, authorities, job action sheets, agreements, and policy constraints that shape a public health emergency response.

Imagine when a new respiratory threat triggers statewide activation.

The response organization must determine which plan applies, who has authority, what local partners are responsible for, which agreements enable staffing and supplies, and what policies constrain data sharing and public communication before the first operational period begins.

Business need and data challenge

Emergency operations plans, annexes, continuity plans, job action sheets, legal authorities, mutual-aid agreements, communication protocols, procurement rules, data-use agreements, and after-action improvements are spread across agencies and repositories. Access and versions often change between exercises and real events.

The business need is a permission-aware, cited view of the policy and plan context for a decision—not an automated command recommendation.

Where the accelerator fits

Direct fit when emergency plans and policies are governed in SharePoint.

The SharePoint knowledge template directly supports authorized semantic retrieval after organizational sign-in and event framing. It can search emergency plans, annexes, agreements, policies, and job action sheets and generate cited briefing content while respecting SharePoint access controls. Documents outside SharePoint need separate secured connectors or links.

Reuse: SharePoint semantic retrieval, inherited permissions, agent synthesis, citations, and report generation.

Adapt: emergency document taxonomy, operational-period prompts, plan owners, legal and policy metadata, partner groups, briefing format, and escalation to command or counsel.

Keep outside: command authority, policy waiver, public warning approval, resource deployment, legal interpretation, and irreversible operational decisions.

Potential outcomes

  • Faster orientation to applicable plans, authorities, and partner roles.
  • More consistent use of cited policy during operational planning.
  • Earlier identification of conflicting responsibilities, expired agreements, and missing decisions.

How this addresses the challenge

Permission-aware retrieval connects the event and jurisdiction to current source documents without creating an uncontrolled emergency-plan copy. Citations, ownership, and effective dates make uncertainty visible and give command, counsel, and program leads a shared starting point.

How this applies across the broader workflow

  • Record the event type, operational period, objectives, affected jurisdictions, and decisions due at activation.
  • Retrieve the emergency operations plan, public health annex, continuity plan, job action sheets, agreements, authorities, data-sharing rules, communication policy, and unresolved after-action items.
  • Use cited evidence to confirm roles, approvals, conflicts, and owners while approved decisions remain in incident logs and action plans rather than the assistant.

Reflection questions

  • Which plans, annexes, agreements, and authorities are operationally binding for each event and jurisdiction, and how will their current version be verified during activation?
  • Which documents can be broadly searchable, which require role-based access, and what restricted operational or legal information must remain outside the shared index?
  • How will the workflow route conflicting plan language, expired agreements, policy exceptions, and unresolved authorities to command, counsel, or the responsible agency before action?
