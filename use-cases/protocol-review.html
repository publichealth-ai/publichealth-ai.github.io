Surveillance policy and protocol review

Prepare policies and protocols for qualified review by finding relevant evidence, changed language, and possible gaps.

Imagine when a new respiratory surveillance feed is ready, but the operating protocol is not.

The program manager must align data collection, access, retention, incident response, quality review, and partner responsibilities before governance reviewers will approve production use.

Business need and data challenge

The evidence is distributed across the draft SOP, data-use agreement, system-security plan, records schedule, privacy requirements, accessibility standards, and earlier approved protocols. Definitions and control language may differ across documents or versions.

The business need is a traceable review package that highlights possible gaps and changed language. AI prepares evidence; qualified reviewers decide.

Potential outcomes

  • Shorter preparation time for multidisciplinary governance review.
  • Traceable findings tied to exact draft text and supporting sources.
  • Clearer release gates for unresolved privacy, security, and program risks.

How this addresses the challenge

The workflow turns scattered source material into a structured review package without treating AI output as approval. Exact passages, versions, owners, and reviewer dispositions make conflicts visible and keep qualified judgment at the center.

How this applies across the broader workflow

  • Ingest the protocol, data-use agreement, security controls, retention schedule, and approved predecessors, then extract structure, defined terms, systems, and roles.
  • Compare purpose, minimum necessary access, data quality, audit, incident response, retention, destruction, and termination language and tie each issue to the exact draft section and source.
  • Capture owner responses and qualified reviewer dispositions in a review matrix while preserving the final protocol, disposition log, and approval evidence as separate records.

Reflection questions

  • Which statutes, policies, standards, agreements, and approved precedents are authoritative for this review, and how will conflicts in scope, date, or precedence be resolved?
  • Who owns each potential finding, which qualified reviewer makes the final disposition, and what evidence must be preserved to explain why the language was accepted or changed?
  • How will unresolved privacy, security, legal, accessibility, records, or program risks block production release rather than becoming advisory notes that are easy to overlook?
