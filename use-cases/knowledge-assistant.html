Epidemiology knowledge assistant

Retrieve current, cited guidance from trusted epidemiology sources without replacing professional judgment or official case determination.

Imagine when a suspected measles case must be classified now.

The laboratory result is available, but the case team must compare the current case definition, vaccination history, symptom timeline, jurisdiction guidance, and required follow-up before deciding whether the case is suspect, probable, or confirmed.

Business need and data challenge

Guidance is spread across national case definitions, state manuals, local procedures, laboratory interpretation guides, SharePoint sites, PDFs, and code-set documentation. Similar titles, superseded versions, and different effective dates create a risk of using the wrong rule.

The business need is rapid access to authoritative evidence with visible citations—not an automated classification decision.

Potential outcomes

  • Faster access to current case definitions and operating guidance.
  • More consistent use of cited evidence across programs and jurisdictions.
  • Visible content gaps revealed by unanswered or low-quality retrievals.

How this addresses the challenge

Version and jurisdiction metadata constrain retrieval to the right source set. Permission checks occur before search, and every answer exposes its passage, owner, and effective date so the case team can compare evidence instead of trusting an unattributed summary.

How this applies across the broader workflow

  • Index approved case definitions, surveillance manuals, investigation SOPs, vaccination guidance, and LOINC or SNOMED references with owner, jurisdiction, effective date, and security metadata.
  • Constrain each case question to authorized sources and retrieve exact passages for clinical criteria, laboratory evidence, epidemiologic linkage, and reporting actions.
  • Have the case team compare cited evidence with the timeline and laboratory data, document its rationale, and make the official determination in the surveillance system.

Reflection questions

  • Which national, state, and local collections are authoritative for each condition, and how will the assistant show when two sources apply to different jurisdictions or effective periods?
  • What content-governance process will approve new material, remove superseded versions, preserve required history, and test that retrieval still returns the correct evidence after every update?
  • Which questions should the assistant decline or escalate because they require senior program, clinical, legal, or case-specific judgment rather than knowledge retrieval?
