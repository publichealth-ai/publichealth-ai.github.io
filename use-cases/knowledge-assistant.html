USE CASE 02 · SURVEILLANCE & INFORMATICS

Epidemiology knowledge assistant

Retrieve current, cited guidance from trusted epidemiology sources without replacing professional judgment or official case determination.

Imagine when an epidemiologist must classify a suspected measles case now.

The laboratory result is available, but the investigator must compare the current case definition, vaccination history, symptom timeline, jurisdiction guidance, and required follow-up before deciding whether the case is suspect, probable, or confirmed.

Business need and data challenge

Guidance is spread across national case definitions, state manuals, local procedures, laboratory interpretation guides, SharePoint sites, PDFs, and code-set documentation. Similar titles, superseded versions, and different effective dates create a risk of using the wrong rule.

The business need is rapid access to authoritative evidence with visible citations—not an automated classification decision.

How this applies across the broader workflow

The assistant indexes approved CDC or CSTE case definitions, state surveillance manuals, local investigation SOPs, vaccination guidance, and LOINC or SNOMED reference material. Metadata records the owner, jurisdiction, effective date, superseded date, audience, and security classification.

When the epidemiologist asks about the case, retrieval is constrained to authorized sources and returns exact passages for clinical criteria, laboratory evidence, epidemiologic linkage, and reporting actions. The investigator compares those passages with the case timeline and laboratory data, documents the rationale, and makes the official determination in the surveillance system.

Questions for your team

  • Who approves content for operational use?
  • How quickly are superseded rules removed from retrieval?
  • Which questions require a program lead or medical director?

Named data and processes

  • Case definitions, SOPs, investigation guides, advisories, LOINC, SNOMED CT, and jurisdiction-specific code sets.
  • Content approval, version control, indexing, retrieval evaluation, case classification, and decision documentation.
  • Owner, effective date, jurisdiction, security group, source URL, citation, and user feedback.

Required guardrails

  • Search only content approved for operational use.
  • Show the source passage, owner, and effective date.
  • Apply role-based access before retrieval.
  • Do not finalize classification or clinical action.
