USE CASE 04

Public health service navigator

Give residents one conversational entry point to current clinics, testing, vaccination, alerts, and other local health services.

Matched code acceleratorVirtual Citizen AssistantOpen GitHub repository

Business need

Turn fragmented services into a clear next step.

Residents often need to understand which program applies, where a service is available, what to bring, whether an appointment is required, and what to do if the service is unavailable. That information may be spread across web pages, PDFs, call centers, and separate scheduling systems.

A service navigator combines trusted content with approved service APIs. It interprets the resident's intent, retrieves current guidance, calls only the necessary plugins, and provides a practical next step with a human handoff when needed.

  • Faster access to the right public health service
  • Fewer transfers and abandoned searches
  • Consistent information across web, chat, and assisted-service channels

Data involved

Combine guidance with live service data.

Service catalog

Program descriptions, intended populations, eligibility guidance, required documents, fees, accessibility, language support, and referral rules.

Location and availability

Clinic locations, hours, service capacity, appointment inventory, walk-in status, geographic coverage, and transportation information.

Public information

Current advisories, testing and vaccination guidance, emergency notices, seasonal campaigns, and approved frequently asked questions.

Operating process

Orchestrate a reliable service journey.

01

Understand

Identify the requested service, location, language, access needs, and urgency.

02

Retrieve

Find the current approved program and service guidance.

03

Connect

Call approved location, schedule, alert, or appointment plugins when needed.

04

Respond

Provide a plain-language next step with source, date, and contact path.

05

Hand off

Escalate urgent, clinical, uncertain, or account-specific requests to staff.

Applied scenarios

Resident journeys the pattern can simplify.

SCENARIO 01

Nearby testing

A resident asks in plain language for testing and receives current locations, hours, appointment requirements, and transportation guidance.

SCENARIO 02

Vaccination access

The navigator uses age, jurisdiction, and service data to show relevant clinics without making a clinical recommendation.

SCENARIO 03

Health alert follow-up

A resident reads an advisory, asks what action applies locally, and receives approved guidance plus the correct contact for questions.

Implementation guardrails

Design for access, safety, and change.

1

Current availability

Separate stable guidance from live location, schedule, capacity, and alert data.

2

No clinical substitution

Do not diagnose, recommend treatment, or replace emergency and clinical channels.

3

Inclusive service

Test accessibility, language quality, digital literacy, and non-digital alternatives with communities.

4

Transparent handoff

Explain when the system cannot complete a task and provide a direct route to human assistance.

Adapt the service-orchestration pattern.

The accelerator includes retrieval-augmented generation, plugin orchestration, contextual conversation, and fallback handling. Replace city-service plugins and content with approved public health services and data sources.
