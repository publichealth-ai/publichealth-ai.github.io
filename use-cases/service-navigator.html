Public health service navigator

Give residents one conversational entry point to current clinics, testing, vaccination, alerts, and other local health services.

Imagine when a resident needs a same-day public health service.

They received a positive home test, do not have a regular provider, need a nearby clinic, and want to know whether an appointment, identification, insurance, or payment is required. The answer is spread across multiple pages and scheduling systems.

Business need and data challenge

Stable program guidance and live operational data behave differently. Eligibility and preparation instructions may change monthly; clinic hours, appointment inventory, walk-in status, and temporary closures can change during the day.

The business need is a single service journey that combines approved guidance with current availability without exposing unnecessary personal data or offering clinical advice.

Potential outcomes

  • Fewer abandoned searches and transfers between service channels.
  • Faster completion of testing, vaccination, and referral journeys.
  • Better visibility into unmet demand and recurring service barriers.

How this addresses the challenge

The orchestration layer combines indexed guidance with live location and appointment data while requesting only the minimum information needed. Explicit fallback and handoff paths prevent an unavailable API or uncertain answer from becoming a dead end.

How this applies across the broader workflow

  • Interpret the requested service, preferred language, approximate location, and accessibility needs, then retrieve approved program and preparation guidance.
  • Query the service directory for clinic type, address, hours, languages, accessibility, eligibility, and jurisdiction, and use an appointment API only when booking is requested.
  • Present current options, source dates, transportation or directions, and the correct contact while routing urgent symptoms, clinical questions, and failed transactions to staff.

Reflection questions

  • Which statewide program facts can be indexed, which local service details must be queried live, and what should the navigator display when an availability or scheduling system is unavailable?
  • What is the minimum location, eligibility, and accessibility information needed to return useful options without collecting identity or health information that the service journey does not require?
  • Which journeys should always preserve a person, phone line, or walk-in path, and how will the experience make that handoff obvious for residents with limited connectivity or digital literacy?
