Public inquiry response assistant

Help contact-center and program staff prepare consistent answers from approved public health content, with citations and clear escalation.

Imagine when a parent calls after receiving a school exposure notice.

The hotline specialist must explain what the notice means, identify symptoms that require clinical attention, describe testing or vaccination resources, and avoid giving advice beyond the health department's approved scope.

Business need and data challenge

Answers depend on the condition, exposure date, jurisdiction, current advisory, age group, language, and available local services. FAQs and scripts may change quickly during an outbreak, while clinic hours and appointment capacity come from separate systems.

The business need is a fast, consistent staff draft with citations and an obvious escalation path for urgent or clinical questions.

Potential outcomes

  • Shorter handling time for routine, approved public health questions.
  • More consistent messages across channels and jurisdictions.
  • Earlier, more reliable escalation of urgent or clinical interactions.

How this addresses the challenge

The workflow separates stable approved language from live service data, exposes the source date, and keeps staff review in the loop. Urgency and scope rules prevent a polished response from bypassing clinical or emergency escalation.

How this applies across the broader workflow

  • Combine exposure notices, approved FAQs, current advisories, jurisdiction guidance, clinic directories, service information, translated scripts, and escalation rules.
  • Identify topic and urgency, retrieve only current approved content, and present a cited draft with the date of each source for staff review.
  • Route urgent symptoms, clinical questions, or possible new cases to the nurse line, epidemiology program, or emergency channel and record the referral reason.

Reflection questions

  • Which symptoms, exposure details, or phrases must trigger immediate escalation, and how will staff confirm that the urgent handoff reached the correct clinical or emergency channel?
  • Who owns each FAQ, advisory, response script, and translated version, and what review cadence is realistic during routine operations versus an active outbreak?
  • How will changes to clinic hours, testing availability, vaccine supply, and jurisdiction routing reach the response tool quickly enough to prevent a cited but operationally incorrect answer?
