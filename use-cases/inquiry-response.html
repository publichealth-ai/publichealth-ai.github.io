USE CASE 03 · COMMUNITY SERVICES

Public inquiry response assistant

Help contact-center and program staff prepare consistent answers from approved public health content, with citations and clear escalation.

Imagine when a parent calls after receiving a school exposure notice.

The hotline specialist must explain what the notice means, identify symptoms that require clinical attention, describe testing or vaccination resources, and avoid giving advice beyond the health department's approved scope.

Business need and data challenge

Answers depend on the condition, exposure date, jurisdiction, current advisory, age group, language, and available local services. FAQs and scripts may change quickly during an outbreak, while clinic hours and appointment capacity come from separate systems.

The business need is a fast, consistent staff draft with citations and an obvious escalation path for urgent or clinical questions.

01Receive

Phone, chat, email, or staff intake form

02Triage

Topic, urgency, location, and language

03Retrieve

Approved advisory, FAQ, and service data

04Review

Specialist verifies the cited draft

05Respond

Answer, refer, or escalate to a clinician

How this applies across the broader workflow

The assistant uses the exposure notice, approved outbreak FAQ, current health advisory, school or jurisdiction guidance, clinic directory, vaccine or testing service information, translated scripts, and escalation rules. It identifies the topic and urgency, then retrieves only current, approved content.

The specialist sees a draft response with citations and the date of each source. If the caller reports urgent symptoms, asks for clinical advice, or provides information that may represent a new case, the workflow routes the interaction to the nurse line, epidemiology program, or emergency channel and records the referral reason.

Data challenges to resolve

  • Synchronizing approved answers across phone, web, chat, email, and translated versions.
  • Combining stable guidance with clinic hours and availability that change during the day.
  • Detecting urgency without retaining more personal information than the service requires.

Required guardrails

  • Assign an owner and review date to every answer source.
  • Define explicit urgent and clinical escalation.
  • Use qualified human review for translated content.
  • Collect only the personal data required for the service.

Reflection questions

  • Which symptoms or phrases trigger immediate escalation?
  • Who owns each FAQ and translated script?
  • How will real-time clinic changes reach the response tool?
