Cross-agency surveillance knowledge hub

Search partner guidance across organizational boundaries without bypassing the permissions attached to each source.

Imagine when a multijurisdictional foodborne cluster crosses three counties.

The statewide investigation needs the current reporting protocol, laboratory submission instructions, interview guidance, partner roles, and escalation contacts. Each item is owned by a different program or agency with different access rules.

Business need and data challenge

Partner knowledge lives in separate SharePoint libraries, Teams-connected sites, portals, and document repositories. Permissions may be inherited from Entra ID groups, while data-use agreements restrict who can see investigation or laboratory procedures.

The business need is unified discovery across authorized sources without copying restricted documents into a broadly accessible index.

Where the accelerator fits

Direct fit when partner knowledge is governed in SharePoint.

The SharePoint knowledge template directly matches permission-aware semantic retrieval after organizational sign-in. It can retrieve authorized policies, plans, procedures, and guidance and return grounded answers with citations. It is not a cross-system data federation layer, so case and laboratory data remain in their systems of record and non-SharePoint repositories need separate secured connectors or links.

Reuse: SharePoint semantic retrieval, inherited access control, agent-based synthesis, citations, and report generation.

Adapt: participating repositories, partner security groups, source-owner metadata, sensitivity handling, audit requirements, and restricted-request escalation.

Keep outside: line lists, laboratory results, identifiable case records, and any operational data that belongs in a controlled system of record.

Potential outcomes

  • Faster discovery of authorized partner procedures and guidance.
  • Fewer unmanaged document copies and permission workarounds.
  • Auditable, citation-backed access across organizational boundaries.

How this addresses the challenge

Retrieval respects source permissions before any content reaches the model, and every answer preserves agency ownership, version, and citation. Restricted operational data stays outside the shared index, reducing both duplication and leakage risk.

How this applies across the broader workflow

  • Authenticate the user and apply SharePoint and source-system permissions to determine which partner libraries, folders, and documents are searchable.
  • Search authorized reporting rules, investigation SOPs, laboratory submission guides, emergency plans, and agreements while preserving agency, owner, effective date, and citation.
  • Keep restricted line lists, laboratory records, and case data in their systems of record, linking authorized users to them without duplicating them into the knowledge index.

Reflection questions

  • Which state, local, laboratory, healthcare, and emergency repositories are authoritative for each topic, and how will conflicting guidance be displayed without erasing source ownership?
  • How are partner access groups requested, approved, reviewed, and revoked, and how quickly will permission changes propagate to search and generated responses?
  • What case, laboratory, partner, or operational information must never enter the shared index, and how will testing prove that restricted content cannot be retrieved or inferred?
